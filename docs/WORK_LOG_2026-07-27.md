## 1. Expanded the market: Central Florida → + Palm Beach County

**The problem.** A new rep was added covering Palm Beach County, but the site described the business as exclusively Central Florida in 26 places. The real issue wasn't word count — geography was load-bearing in the sales argument. Four lines made *focus itself* the differentiator:

- "**100%** Dedicated to the Central Florida Market"
- "We don't split attention across the state. Central Florida is our entire focus."
- "If your brand isn't in Central Florida, it isn't truly in Florida."
- Central Florida is "the single most consequential market **in the state**"

A find-and-replace would have turned the strongest differentiator into a contradiction, and would have made the 38% liquor-sales stat argue *against* the new territory.

**The approach.** Kept the focus argument and made it bigger: from "one market, total focus" to "two complementary markets, a dedicated operator in each."

|  | Central Florida | Palm Beach County |
|---|---|---|
| **Delivers** | Volume, tourism throughput | Price point, prestige, private clubs |
| **Proves to a brand** | Your brand moves | Your brand belongs on the top shelf |

**Key rewrites**

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "100% — Central Florida is our entire focus" | "**2** — Two markets, a dedicated operator in each" |
| "If your brand isn't in Central Florida…" | "**Orlando proves your volume. Palm Beach proves your price point.**" |
| "100% Dedicated to the Central Florida Market" | "**2** Florida Markets. A Dedicated Operator in Each." |
| "38% of Florida's Liquor Sales…" (stat bar) | "**86M** Annual Visitors Across Our Two Markets" |
| "Why Central Florida Is the Market Your Brand Needs" | "**Two Markets. The Whole Florida Opportunity.**" |
| "Ready to Own Central Florida?" | "**Ready to Own the Room?**" |

The 38% figure wasn't deleted — it moved into body copy, reframed as Central Florida being the state's *volume engine* rather than the only market that matters.

In the figures grid the two markets are paired **row-by-row on the same metric** so they read as a direct comparison: $94.5B vs $11.7B economic impact, then $164M vs $21M daily visitor spend, then two statewide category stats as shared context.

### Say "Palm Beach County" — never "South Florida"

The rep covers Palm Beach County only, not Miami-Dade or Broward. "South Florida" would be an overclaim a brand can check, and would generate Miami/Fort Lauderdale inquiries that can't be served. **Zero occurrences of "South Florida" on the site — keep it that way.**

Bare "Palm Beach" was also tightened to "Palm Beach County" in the intro, because "Palm Beach" alone reads as the *Town* of Palm Beach (~9,000 residents) rather than a county running from Boca Raton to Jupiter.

### Palm Beach figures — sourcing

From Discover The Palm Beaches, press release dated 12 Feb 2026, **calendar year 2025**:

- **10.7M visitors** (up from 9.9M in 2024, +8%) — first year above 10M
- **$7.7B visitor spending** → the $21M/day figure on the site
- **$11.7B total economic impact**
- ~95,000 jobs supported

Two caveats to remember:
1. Spending and economic impact are labelled **"preliminary estimate"** at the source and may be revised.
2. The **86M combined visitor stat mixes years** — Central Florida 2024 (75.3M) + Palm Beach 2025 (10.7M). Each is its market's latest reported annual figure, which is normal practice, but it is not a single-year number.

## 2. SEO foundation

The site previously had **no** structured data, Open Graph tags, sitemap, robots.txt, canonical tags, or favicon.

**Honest framing:** for a B2B spirits brokerage, chasing keyword rankings is mostly a losing game — search volume for terms like "spirit broker Orlando" is close to zero. The value is in being findable by name, credible when checked out, correctly understood by AI assistants that brands use to shortlist vendors, and sharp-looking when the link is pasted into a LinkedIn DM. That last one is where deal flow actually comes from.

### What was added

- **Open Graph + Twitter Card tags** on both pages, with a generated 1200×630 preview image (`images/og-image.jpg`) built from the hero photo with the wordmark in Garamond. Pasting the URL now renders a card instead of a bare link.
- **JSON-LD structured data** — `ProfessionalService` + `WebSite` on the homepage declaring `areaServed` (Central Florida, Orange County, Palm Beach County), all six services via `hasOfferCatalog`, and the social profiles. `ImageGallery` on the gallery page.
- **sitemap.xml**, **robots.txt**, **canonical tags**, **favicon set** (`favicon.ico` at 16/32/48px, `apple-touch-icon.png`, `icon-512.png`), and Google Fonts `preconnect` hints.

## 3. Performance — the biggest actual win

The homepage was shipping **10.22 MB of images**. Eight were over 1 MB each; the six service card photos were 1376×768 but weighed 1.1–1.35 MB apiece because they'd been saved at near-lossless compression. That's a 15–30 second load on a phone.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage image payload | 10.22 MB | **1.64 MB** |
| Initial mobile load | 10.26 MB | **462 KB** |
| Hero image | 1,410 KB | **401 KB** |
| Images with width/height | 0 of 26 | **26 of 26** |
| Lazy-loaded | 0 | **24** |

Each image was resized against its *measured* display size rather than a guess. For the hero, five settings were tested against a lossless same-size reference: **2200px @ q82** won at 401 KB with PSNR 34.5 — only 0.9 dB below a 607 KB variant, and it sits under a 0.30–0.82 alpha overlay that masks the difference.

`width`/`height` attributes reserve space during load (fixes Cumulative Layout Shift); lazy-loading everything below the fold is why the initial load is 462 KB rather than 1.64 MB. The hero is marked `fetchpriority="high"`.

## 4. Bugs found and fixed

**Hero image was 404ing in production.** The page requested `images/hero.jpg` but the file is `images/Hero.jpg`. Worked on case-insensitive local disk, broke on the live host — so the hero had likely been missing in production for a while.

**Contact email was dead.** The HTML had been processed by Cloudflare at some point, which obfuscated the address and left a reference to Cloudflare's decoder script. The site runs on **Netlify with no Cloudflare in front**, so that script 404s — every visitor saw the literal text `[email protected]` linking to a 404. On a lead-generation site that's the primary contact method gone. Restored as a plain `mailto:info@ihospitality.vip` and removed the orphaned script tag.

**Hero paragraph failed accessibility contrast.** It was set to 75% opacity, `font-weight:300`, and had *no* text-shadow while the eyebrow above it did. Measured by compositing the real hero image with the overlay and computing WCAG ratios per pixel: **29.1% of the paragraph area was below the AA 4.5:1 threshold**, bottoming out at 1.85:1 over the lit bottles.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Average contrast | 5.64:1 | **9.14:1** |
| Worst case | 1.85:1 | **3.09:1** |
| Area below AA | 29.1% | **5.2%** |

Fixed with opacity 0.95, weight 400, a text-shadow, and raising the overlay mid-stop from 0.45 to 0.62.

**Eyebrow orphaned a word.** The longer "Palm Beach County" string wrapped badly between 485–600px, leaving a 67px orphan under a 372px line. Tapered letter-spacing to `clamp(1.5px, 0.35vw, 3px)`; desktop stays at exactly 3px.

### ⚠️ The `aspect-ratio` trap — worth remembering

Adding `width`/`height` attributes **silently broke three image crops.** HTML `width`/`height` map to CSS `width`/`height` as *presentational hints*, and when both are definite the browser **ignores `aspect-ratio` entirely**.

| Element | Intended | Became |
|---|---|---|
| Service cards ×6 | 370×208 (16:9) | 370×447 |
| Market photo | 509×636 (4:5) | 509×1152 |
| About photo | 501×667 (3:4) | 501×1600 |

**Fix:** add `height:auto` to any rule that sizes an image purely via `aspect-ratio`. The author stylesheet then beats the attribute hint, and the CLS benefit of the attributes is retained.

```css
.service-card img   { width:100%; height:auto; aspect-ratio:16/9; object-fit:cover; }
.market-img img     { width:100%; height:auto; aspect-ratio:4/5;  object-fit:cover; }
.about-img-wrap img { width:100%; height:auto; aspect-ratio:3/4;  object-fit:cover; }
```

Any future image with an `aspect-ratio` rule needs the same treatment.

| **Forms** | Netlify Forms (`data-netlify="true"`, form name `partnership`) |
| **Deploy** | Push to `main` → Netlify builds in ~15s |

**DNS records go in Netlify, not Dynadot.** Domain management → click the *Netlify DNS* link on the domain row → Add new record. Leave the **Name field blank** for apex records; entering `ihospitality.vip` creates `ihospitality.vip.ihospitality.vip`, which looks fine in the table and never verifies.

### Two gotchas hit during this work

- **PowerShell 5.1 mangles UTF-8.** `Get-Content -Raw` reads as ANSI and `Set-Content -Encoding utf8` writes back mojibake (`—` → `â€"`). Use `[System.IO.File]::ReadAllText($p,[System.Text.Encoding]::UTF8)` and `WriteAllText` with `UTF8Encoding($false)`.
- **Google Search Console sitemap field differs by property type.** A *Domain* property (DNS-verified) needs the **full URL**; only a *URL-prefix* property takes the path alone. "Couldn't fetch" immediately after submitting is normal — it resolved on reload.
